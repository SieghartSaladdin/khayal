# Website copy

Good copy says what the product does, for whom, and the evidence. Bad copy could be pasted onto any product.

Write the copy in the language the site is for (Indonesian or English), and reply to the user in their language.

## Rules

- **Headlines name the product's job.** "Send invoices before they are due" beats "Supercharge your workflow with AI".
- **Buttons name the action.** "Send your first invoice" beats "Get Started", "Learn More", or "Explore".
- **Do not invent facts.** No numbers, customer names, testimonials, dates, security claims, or performance claims that are not in the source or given by the user. If one is needed and missing, write `[REAL DATA]` or make no claim. An empty section beats a fabricated one.
- **Cut empty words.** Revolutionary, seamless, next-gen, cutting-edge, AI-powered, unlock, elevate, game-changer. Replace them with something visible or measurable.
- **Name the actor.** "We rewrote the pricing page" beats "The pricing page was updated". Avoid giving objects human verbs ("the dashboard understands what you need").
- **Avoid AI rhythm.** A forced list of three, "not just X but also Y", runs of short fragments for drama, empty ranges ("from start to finish"), and upbeat endings with no content.
- **No em dashes.** Use a period, comma, colon, or rewrite the sentence.
- **No decorative emoji** in headings, bullets, or buttons.
- **No quotation marks or all-caps for emphasis.** Write the emphasis into the sentence.
- **Only the sections the product needs.** An FAQ contains only questions users really ask about this product.

## Do not overdo it

Removing AI tells is only half the job. Text with no voice also reads as machine-made. If the user gives a sample of their own writing, match its habits (sentence length, vocabulary, punctuation), except for the rules above, which you raise with the user if their sample breaks them.

## Example

Before: "Unlock the power of next-gen automation and take your business to the next level."
After: "Customers get a reminder on WhatsApp and email, so you never retype a follow-up."

(The product in this example is fictional. Replacement sentences must always come from the real product or from the user.)

## Loop: draft, audit, final

1. **Draft.** Write with the rules above. Does it sound like a person when read aloud?
2. **Audit.** Answer two questions briefly: what makes this text obviously AI-written, and does it state any fact, name, number, or date that is not in the source?
3. **Final.** Revise based on the answers, then search once more for em dashes and invented claims. A single hit means it is not done.
